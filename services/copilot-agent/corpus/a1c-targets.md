## Summary

Hemoglobin A1c (A1c) estimates average blood glucose over roughly the prior
two to three months. Target A1c is individualized, but a widely used
ADA-style general target for most non-pregnant adults with diabetes is an
A1c below 7%. This is an educational summary for retrieval testing, not
clinical advice.

## Target Ranges

- Most non-pregnant adults with diabetes: A1c target generally below 7%.
- Healthy adults with a long life expectancy and low hypoglycemia risk: a
  stricter target, below 6.5%, may be reasonable if it can be reached safely.
- Older adults, or those with limited life expectancy, significant
  comorbidity, or a history of severe hypoglycemia: a looser target, such as
  below 8% or 8.5%, is often more appropriate.
- Pregnant patients with pre-existing or gestational diabetes have distinct,
  tighter glycemic targets that are outside the scope of this summary.

## Monitoring Frequency

- Adults meeting their target with stable therapy: check A1c about twice a
  year.
- Adults not at target, or with a recent therapy change: check A1c about
  every three months until stable.

## Interpreting Trends

A rising A1c trend across visits, even while still numerically "in range,"
is generally more clinically informative than a single value in isolation —
it can signal declining adherence, disease progression, or a need for
therapy escalation before the value crosses the target threshold.
