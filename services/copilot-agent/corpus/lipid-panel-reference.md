## Summary

A standard lipid panel reports total cholesterol, LDL cholesterol, HDL
cholesterol, and triglycerides. Commonly cited general reference
categories are summarized below; individualized treatment targets (e.g. for
a patient with established cardiovascular disease) are typically lower and
are set by risk category, not by these general population ranges. This is
an educational summary for retrieval testing, not clinical advice.

## General Reference Categories

- Total cholesterol: desirable below 200 mg/dL; borderline-high 200-239
  mg/dL; high 240 mg/dL or above.
- LDL cholesterol: optimal below 100 mg/dL; near-optimal 100-129 mg/dL;
  borderline-high 130-159 mg/dL; high 160-189 mg/dL; very high 190 mg/dL or
  above.
- HDL cholesterol: low (higher risk) below 40 mg/dL in men or below 50
  mg/dL in women; higher HDL is generally considered protective.
- Triglycerides: normal below 150 mg/dL; borderline-high 150-199 mg/dL;
  high 200-499 mg/dL; very high 500 mg/dL or above.

## Fasting Considerations

A non-fasting lipid panel is acceptable for general screening in most
adults; triglycerides are the value most affected by recent food intake, so
a markedly elevated non-fasting triglyceride level is often confirmed with
a fasting recheck before major treatment decisions.

## Follow-Up Cadence

- Adults not on lipid-lowering therapy with a normal panel: rescreen every
  4-6 years is a commonly cited general interval, adjusted for individual
  risk factors.
- Adults recently started or adjusted on statin therapy: a follow-up panel
  at roughly 4-12 weeks (see `statin-monitoring`) to assess response, then
  periodically thereafter once stable.
