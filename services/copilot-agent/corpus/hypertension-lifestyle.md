## Summary

For most adults with elevated blood pressure or Stage 1 hypertension
without additional high-risk features, lifestyle modification is a
first-line intervention, often trialed before or alongside pharmacotherapy.
See `blood-pressure-categories` for category definitions. This is an
educational summary for retrieval testing, not clinical advice.

## Common Lifestyle Interventions

- Dietary pattern emphasizing vegetables, fruit, whole grains, and reduced
  saturated fat (a DASH-style pattern) is commonly cited as lowering
  systolic pressure meaningfully in adherent patients.
- Sodium reduction, commonly targeted around 1,500-2,300 mg/day depending on
  the source guidance.
- Regular aerobic physical activity, commonly cited around 90-150
  minutes/week.
- Weight reduction where applicable — even modest weight loss is commonly
  associated with a measurable blood-pressure reduction.
- Limiting alcohol intake and, where relevant, smoking cessation support.

## Follow-Up Cadence

- Elevated blood pressure or Stage 1 hypertension managed with lifestyle
  alone: recheck at roughly 3-6 months to assess response before deciding
  whether to add pharmacotherapy.
- Once on stable antihypertensive therapy at target: routine follow-up
  roughly every 3-6 months is a commonly cited cadence, more frequent if
  therapy was recently changed or control is not yet at target.

## When Pharmacotherapy Is Added Sooner

Stage 2 hypertension, or Stage 1 hypertension with additional
cardiovascular risk factors or established cardiovascular disease, commonly
prompts starting pharmacotherapy alongside lifestyle measures rather than
trialing lifestyle changes alone first.
