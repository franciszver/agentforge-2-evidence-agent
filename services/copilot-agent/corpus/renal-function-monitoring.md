## Summary

Several common medication classes carry a renal-function monitoring
expectation because they can reduce renal perfusion or have renally-cleared,
dose-dependent toxicity. This is an educational summary for retrieval
testing, not clinical advice.

## ACE Inhibitors and ARBs

A baseline creatinine/eGFR and potassium is typically checked before
starting an ACE inhibitor or ARB, with a recheck at roughly 1-2 weeks after
starting or up-titrating. A rise in creatinine up to about 30% from baseline
is commonly considered an expected, acceptable hemodynamic effect rather
than a reason to stop; a larger rise, or a significant potassium increase,
generally prompts reassessment of the regimen. See `nsaid-interactions` for
the added risk when an NSAID is also present.

## NSAIDs (Chronic Use)

Chronic NSAID use, especially in older adults, patients with reduced
baseline kidney function, or those on an ACE inhibitor/ARB or diuretic, is a
common reason to periodically check renal function; short, occasional NSAID
use in an otherwise healthy adult is a much lower concern.

## Metformin

Metformin is renally cleared, and reduced kidney function raises the risk
of drug accumulation and lactic acidosis. eGFR is generally checked at
least annually in patients on metformin, more frequently if renal function
is borderline or trending down, with dose adjustment or discontinuation
considered at defined eGFR thresholds.

## Diuretics

Loop and thiazide diuretics can cause volume depletion, which compounds the
renal-perfusion risk of the interactions described above; a basic metabolic
panel (creatinine, potassium) is commonly checked periodically, and sooner
after a dose change.
