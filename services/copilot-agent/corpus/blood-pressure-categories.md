## Summary

Blood pressure is generally described in categories rather than a single
cutoff, since risk rises along a continuum. This summary uses a JNC/ACC-AHA
-style category scheme for adults, based on the higher of the systolic or
diastolic reading. This is an educational summary for retrieval testing, not
clinical advice.

## Categories

- Normal: systolic below 120 mmHg AND diastolic below 80 mmHg.
- Elevated: systolic 120-129 mmHg AND diastolic below 80 mmHg.
- Stage 1 hypertension: systolic 130-139 mmHg OR diastolic 80-89 mmHg.
- Stage 2 hypertension: systolic 140 mmHg or higher OR diastolic 90 mmHg or
  higher.
- Hypertensive crisis: systolic higher than 180 mmHg AND/OR diastolic higher
  than 120 mmHg — this requires prompt clinical attention, especially if
  accompanied by symptoms such as chest pain, shortness of breath, or
  neurologic changes.

## Confirmation Before Diagnosis

A single elevated office reading does not establish a hypertension
diagnosis. Confirmation typically relies on an average of two or more
readings on two or more separate occasions, or out-of-office measurement
(home or ambulatory monitoring) to rule out white-coat effect.

## Trend Interpretation

For a patient already diagnosed with hypertension, a documented reading
that has moved down a full category since the prior visit (e.g. Stage 2 to
Stage 1) is generally a meaningful positive trend worth noting in a
pre-visit summary, distinct from ordinary reading-to-reading variability.
