## Summary

Statins (e.g. atorvastatin, simvastatin, rosuvastatin) are a first-line
lipid-lowering therapy. Routine monitoring is lighter than it was in older
practice, but a few checks remain standard. This is an educational summary
for retrieval testing, not clinical advice.

## Baseline Liver Function

A baseline liver panel (e.g. ALT) is generally obtained before starting a
statin. Routine ongoing liver-enzyme monitoring in an asymptomatic patient
is usually not needed; liver enzymes are checked if the patient develops
symptoms suggestive of hepatotoxicity (e.g. unexplained fatigue, jaundice,
right-upper-quadrant pain) or if clinically indicated for another reason.

## Muscle Symptoms and CK

Statins carry a known risk of myopathy. Routine creatine kinase (CK)
monitoring in an asymptomatic patient is generally not recommended; a CK
level is checked if the patient reports new muscle pain, tenderness, or
weakness. Persistent, unexplained muscle symptoms with a significantly
elevated CK generally warrant holding the statin and reassessing.

## Interaction Caution: CYP3A4 Inhibitors

Certain statins metabolized through CYP3A4 (e.g. simvastatin, atorvastatin)
have a well-known interaction with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g. certain
macrolide antibiotics, azole antifungals, some protease inhibitors) — the
combination can raise statin levels and myopathy risk. Dose adjustment or
temporary interruption is often considered when a short course of a strong
interacting drug is needed.

## Lipid Panel Follow-Up

After starting or adjusting statin therapy, a follow-up lipid panel at
roughly 4-12 weeks is a common practice to assess response before further
titration; see `lipid-panel-reference` for reference ranges and longer-term
follow-up cadence.
