## Summary

Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) — for example ibuprofen,
naproxen, and diclofenac — are common over-the-counter and prescription
analgesics with several well-known interaction cautions, largely because
they reduce renal prostaglandin synthesis and inhibit platelet function.
This is an educational summary for common interaction cautions for
retrieval testing, not clinical advice.

## ACE Inhibitors and ARBs

Combining an NSAID with an ACE inhibitor (e.g. lisinopril) or an
angiotensin-receptor blocker (e.g. losartan) can blunt the blood-pressure-
lowering effect and, more importantly, can reduce renal perfusion — the
combination is sometimes described as part of a "triple whammy" when a
diuretic is also present. Caution is generally warranted, especially in
older adults, patients with reduced kidney function, or those who are
volume-depleted; renal function is often checked if the combination is used
for more than a short course.

## Diuretics

NSAIDs can blunt the effect of loop and thiazide diuretics and, combined
with volume depletion, add to the renal-perfusion concern described above
for ACE inhibitors/ARBs.

## Anticoagulants and Antiplatelets

NSAIDs inhibit platelet function and can irritate the GI mucosa, so
combining an NSAID with an anticoagulant (e.g. warfarin, a DOAC) or another
antiplatelet agent (e.g. aspirin, clopidogrel) raises bleeding risk,
particularly GI bleeding. This combination generally warrants caution and,
where feasible, an alternative analgesic.

## Lithium

NSAIDs can reduce renal lithium clearance, raising lithium levels toward
toxicity; this combination generally calls for closer lithium-level
monitoring if it cannot be avoided.

## Methotrexate

At higher (e.g. oncologic) methotrexate doses, NSAIDs can reduce renal
clearance and increase methotrexate toxicity risk; at typical
low-dose/weekly regimens the interaction is usually less pronounced but
still worth noting.
